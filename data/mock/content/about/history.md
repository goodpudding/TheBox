# How The Box got here

The Box started because Burien needed a public shop. Debra George, Discover Burien’s executive director, watched a Farmers Market vendor struggle to make enough of their hand-crafted boxes to sell. The city had makers. It did not have a room with the tools.

In 2021, Discover Burien cleared a former classroom on the lower level of 611 SW 152nd Street, bought equipment, and hired staff — the Highline area’s first publicly accessible makerspace. Soft opening was October 2021. A grand opening followed on March 16, 2022.

Denise Ferguson, a long-time Discover Burien employee and Burien small-business owner, laid out the floor: work areas by craft, machines grouped so you can learn one station and then the next. Mitchell Allen, The Box’s makerspace manager, came from museums and after-school programs. His job, as he put it, is to be the catalyst — get people to his level on a machine, then let the community outrun him.

The original brief was a launchpad for micro-businesses: small production runs, prototypes, branded merch. It is also a place to make for its own sake — costumes, cribbage boards, a first 3D print. Membership, certifications, and classes live in this portal now. The rest of Discover Burien’s public story stays on discoverburien.org.
